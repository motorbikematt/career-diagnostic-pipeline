# LinkedIn and Recruiter Guidance (2026): Master Rules and resume-fit Gap Analysis

Status: advice and action. This file consolidates two LinkedIn-hosted broadcasts into formal rules for three surfaces (the Work History Document, the resume, and job description evaluation), plus a LinkedIn profile section, and compares those rules with what the `resume-fit` skill does today. No code, contract, schema or template in `.claude/skills/resume-fit/` has been changed. Proposed changes are tracked in `TODO.md` items 32 to 41.

Prepared: 2026-10-22. Companion files: `ats-evidence-2026.md` (evidence, owns the S-numbered sources) and `resume-fit-guidance.md` (format and content rules, rule audit).

## 2. Master guidance

### 2.1 Positioning (all surfaces)

| ID | Rule | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Present the profile and resume as a statement of where you are going, not a record of where you have been. | Official (LinkedIn) | W1 |
| P2 | Put the target role title where the reader looks first: the headline, and the line under the name on a resume. A recruiter with seven seconds should see the target title before anything else. | Official (LinkedIn, recruiter) | W1, W2 |
| P3 | One direction per profile. For two related targets, keep two resumes (one per target title) and one profile aligned to where they overlap. Never create two profiles. | Official (LinkedIn) | W1 |
| P4 | Narrow many possible targets to one or two. Test each by the overlap of what you enjoy, what you can do, and what the market demands. | Host-endorsed | W2 |
| P5 | For a pivot, use the summary or About section as a bridge: say who you are, what you did, how, and the proof, then tie it to the target job. Treat past experience as added value, not as something to hide. | Official (LinkedIn, recruiter) | W1, W2 |
| P6 | Learn the titles the market actually uses by reading current job listings, then use them in the headline and profile. Titles change over time, and a stale title can mislabel you. | Host-endorsed | W2 |
| P7 | Scope versus title: the recruiter's first diagnostic is whether the candidate has the scope but not the title. The recommended fix is a resume title that speaks the target's language, with the true title given at the background check. **DECISION D2.** | Official (recruiter) | W2 |

### 2.2 Work History Document (WHD) guidance

The broadcasts do not mention a WHD. These rules say what a WHD must hold so that the resume and profile rules above can be followed without invention. They are derived from the sources, not quoted.

| ID | Rule | Derived from |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Record scope facts per role: team size, number of sites, budget or P&L, revenue, users. Scope is the "what" in the summary formula and the line under each role title. | P5, R2, R7 |
| H2 | Record the title on the offer letter and in HR records as the title of record. The resume and profile may use a truthful parenthetical or market-equivalent title; the title of record is what a background check will confirm. | P7 |
| H3 | Record outcomes as number plus method. A missing measure is recorded as an open question, never invented. | R7; W2 shows bracketed placeholders for unknown measures |
| H4 | Keep the WHD exhaustive. It is the master record from which every tailored resume is trimmed. | R11 |
| H5 | Keep voice-sample answers in the candidate's own words. Authenticity beats polish on every surface. | C3 |
| H6 | Record education dates and GPA, but mark them so drafts can omit them under rule R9. | R9 |
| H7 | Record side projects, volunteer work, commissions and talks (the "Beyond Employment" block). They feed the About section, the Featured section and the bridge summary. | L3, L4 |

### 2.3 Resume guidance

| ID | Rule | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Banner line under the name: target title, then two or three keyword phrases drawn from the JD (function, scale, industries). | Official (recruiter) | W2 |
| R2 | Summary formula: who, what, how, proof. Example shape: role, years, scope with numbers, a known strength, one headline result. | Official (recruiter) | W2 |
| R3 | Remove subjective filler ("detail-oriented," "professional") unless you can quantify it. Keep only what can be verified. | Official (recruiter) | W2 |
| R4 | The first two-thirds of page one (banner, summary, core competencies, current role) is the scan zone. Spend the effort there. | Official (recruiter) | W2 |
| R5 | Core competencies: realign them to the JD, most impactful first. Every keyword needs proof in a bullet; a keyword with no quantified use is worthless once a human reads it. | Official (recruiter) | W2 |
| R6 | Mirror the JD's wording where it is true (for example "stakeholder management" and not only "customer relationships"). Read the top third to two-thirds of the JD's requirements as the core ask. | Official (recruiter) | W2 |
| R7 | Lead each role with a scope line. Write bullets as result plus method ("by standardizing execution, resetting expectations, coaching daily"). | Official (recruiter) | W2 |
| R8 | Skip soft-skill lists on a resume; you cannot prove them there. | Official (recruiter) | W2 |
| R9 | To reduce age bias: after about five years of experience, drop graduation years and GPA. Skip optional date fields. If an application field is required, answer truthfully. | Official (recruiter) | W1, W2 |
| R10 | Optimize for the ATS and the human. Do not use hidden or one-point white text or prompt injection; the guest says it does not work. | Official (recruiter) | W2 |
| R11 | Keep a master resume of everything you have done; tailor a copy for each application (about five minutes once the master exists). | Official (recruiter) | W2 |
| R12 | Cover letters are mostly unread by recruiters. The summary does the cover letter's job. If you write one, address the hiring manager, or send the same text as a message. | Official (recruiter), Host-endorsed | W2 |
| R13 | Never claim experience you lack. Embellish framing, not facts: where you worked, what school, and what experience are not negotiable. | Official (recruiter) | W2 |

| ID | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|

## 3. Worked examples (patterns, not evidence)

The broadcasts rewrote four items live. Their changes show the rules in use. Names are omitted.

| Case | Before | After | Rules shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resume, district manager to director | Generic leader banner; adjective-heavy summary; five dated highlight bullets; three-column competency grid; true title | Target-title banner; who/what/how/proof summary with numbers; one-line competencies in JD terms; scope line; bullets with method; title changed to the target's | P2, P5, P7, R1 to R7 |
| Headline and About, film assistant director to production manager | Current role first; buzzwords; one dense paragraph | Target first, credential named, quantified scope; five short first-person paragraphs; explicit ask; core strengths line | P2, L1, L2 |
| Headline and About, interior designer to design research | Topic-led headline; abstract narrative; no stated target | Target title first; one-sentence opener; employer paragraph moved up; side project added; closing names target roles | P1, P5, L1, L2 |
| Post, engineering executive | Short post that names a product and a job card | Slide advice: context in the hook, expand why the product is good, conversation-starting call to action | C2 |

**Risk pattern worth carrying into the skill.** Several "after" versions added facts the "before" did not state: a scope word upgraded from "portfolio" to "P&L," a new initiative ("product relocation plan"), a new stakeholder type, a years figure changed, and "co-led" changed to "led." The coaches did this in a live demo with the candidate present. `resume-fit` forbids it without a WHD source (finishing invariants, prescription Add rule), which is the safer design.

## 5. Decisions needed

**D1: Summary and banner policy. DECIDED and IMPLEMENTED 2026-10-22 (TODO 32).** The credentialed-title limit in point 4 was confirmed by the owner.
1. Every resume has a summary. If the seed resume has one, synthesis rebuilds it to the formula in point 2; if not, synthesis prescribes an Add. This replaces the finishing-contract rule "do not add a summary unless one exists" and the guidance rule S4 ("a summary is optional").
2. One summary, one formula. The "bridge summary" of [W2] is not a separate section. It is the same summary built as who, what, how, proof, where the proof sentence ties the experience to this JD. Before this decision the skill only reframed lines of whatever summary the seed resume held ("what it signals now, what it needs to signal") and had no content formula. The bridge element matters most for a pivot, mixed background or title gap, but it applies on every run.
3. Constraints kept from `resume-fit-guidance.md` section 7: every claim traces to an approved bullet (until the bullet library exists, to a WHD anchor), two to four lines, no first person, no objective statement. The summary is protected in the length round; cuts come from elsewhere first.
4. Banner line: the JD's title verbatim, as an owner-approved exception to the no-fabrication rule, because its purpose is to tie the resume to the posting. Scope of the exception: the banner only. Role-entry titles stay true (D2 remains open for them). The honesty check records it as a "banner exception," and the Integrity Verdict still states the true title. Confirmed limit: the exception does not apply where the title implies a license or credential (engineer, nurse, CPA and similar).
5. Authorship: synthesis drafts two or three summary options from approved bullets; the user picks or edits; the user's edits are stored verbatim and never re-polished. The voice-sample check still applies to every option.

**D2: Title policy.** The recruiter's rule (P7) is to change the resume title to the target's and give the true title at the background check. This conflicts with the no-fabrication invariant, and verification can fail at HR records, references or the offer stage.
- Option A (recommended): default to a truthful parenthetical that carries the scope ("Regional Operations Manager (director-level scope: 11 sites, 100+ associates)"), which gives the keyword and the bridge without a mismatch.
- Option B: allow a title swap only when the user ratifies it in the Stretch round, record it in the WHD (`title_on_record` stays true), and put a background-check reminder in the report.
- Option C: never allow a swap.

**D3: LinkedIn scope.** Whether `resume-fit` gains a profile mode, or a separate skill is created for it. The profile mode needs the same honesty guardrails and voice sample, so a mode inside this skill reuses the most.
